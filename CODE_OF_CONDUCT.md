# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume good faith. Critique ideas, not people.

## What's expected

- **Argue about the work.** Disagreement is welcome and useful — this project has
  strong opinions about staying small, and pressure-testing them is the point. Aim it
  at the design, not at whoever proposed it.
- **Assume competence and good faith.** People arrive with different context. A
  question that seems obvious to you usually isn't.
- **Accept a "no" gracefully.** Most feature proposals here get declined on the
  grounds that they don't clearly pay for themselves — see
  [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). That is a judgment about scope, not about you.
- **Give a "no" gracefully too.** That applies to me as maintainer more than anyone.

## What's not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, demeaning or discriminatory comments, sustained
disruption, publishing others' private information, and unwelcome sexual attention.
None of it is tolerated regardless of who it comes from or how good their patches are.

## Scope

Applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any other project
space, including when representing the project elsewhere.

## Enforcement

Report by emailing **snir.orlanczyk@gmail.com**, or by using GitHub's "Report content"
on the offending item if you would rather not contact me directly. Reports are handled
privately, and I won't share reporter details without permission.

Responses range from a request to change behavior, to editing or removing content, to
blocking from the repository — proportionate to what happened and whether it continues.

This is a one-maintainer project, so enforcement is one person's judgment. I'll aim to
be fair, and to be quicker to act on repeated behavior than on a single bad day.
